# A Guide to Alexandria's Commercial Areas

## Introduction

Alexandria is known for its wide variety of commercial areas, with each area
differing in population density, income level, foot traffic, and the type
of businesses common there. That's why choosing the right area is one of the
most important factors for the success of any commercial venture.

This guide provides an overview of Alexandria's most well-known commercial
areas, clarifying the businesses best suited to each area, its most notable
streets known for specific activities, and the general income level in each
area.

## General Framework: Income Levels Across Alexandria's Areas

Before detailing each area, it's useful to know that Alexandria's
residential/commercial districts generally fall into three tiers, which
guides any location recommendation for any business type:

- **Upper-tier districts**: Smouha, San Stefano, Roushdy, Azarita, Shatby,
  Stanley, Corniche El Montazah, El Maamoura -- suitable for modern/premium
  retail and higher-end cafés and restaurants.
- **Upper-middle districts**: Kafr Abdo, Gleem, Sidi Gaber, Fleming, upper
  Montazah -- a good balance between purchasing power and foot traffic,
  suitable for cafés, restaurants, and mid-to-premium retail.
- **Middle-tier districts**: Miami, Mandara, Sidi Bishr, Asafra, Agami, El
  Bitash, Amreya, Khorshid, Camp Cesar -- best suited for everyday-needs
  businesses (supermarkets, pharmacies, mid-market clothing, phone shops)
  where volume and repeat business matter more than premium positioning.

Matching a business's price point to the district's income level is one of
the single biggest factors in a shop's success in Alexandria -- a premium
boutique on a middle-tier residential street, or a bargain outlet on a
premium street, both tend to underperform relative to expectations.

## First: Downtown (Mahatet Raml)

### Overview
Mahatet Raml is one of Alexandria's most famous commercial areas, known for
heavy foot traffic throughout the day, and home to many shops, restaurants,
and cafés. The area stretching between Mahatet Raml and Mahatet Masr
(Downtown) includes several notable streets, each with a different
character:

- **Fouad Street** -- one of the oldest and busiest streets in the city,
  with a dense retail, café, and restaurant strip, many venues known for
  extended or effectively 24-hour operating hours. Best fit: clothing
  retail, cafés, restaurants wanting to capture late-night trade.
- **Safeya Zaghloul Street** -- close to Mahatet Raml, a mix of restaurants,
  cafés, and retail; a profile similar to Fouad Street but more mixed-use.
- **Nabi Daniel Street** -- one of Alexandria's oldest streets, historically
  known as a used-book market, currently under a municipal heritage
  redevelopment, also home to gift and accessory shops. Best fit: books/
  stationery, gifts, specialty retail with a cultural angle. Less suited to
  a restaurant needing a large kitchen footprint (the buildings are older
  and relatively narrow).
- **Attarine** -- a traditional commercial district near Downtown,
  historically tied to the spice, herb, and textile trade, still associated
  with traditional crafts. Best fit: a herbal/spice shop, or a tailor
  benefiting from the district's craft character.

### Suitable Businesses
Clothing stores, shoes, accessories, restaurants, cafés, bookstores, gifts,
herbal/spice shops (Attarine specifically).

### Advantages
- High traffic density.
- Heavy foot traffic.
- Commercial fame.
- Easy access by public transport.

### Challenges
- High rents.
- Heavy congestion.
- Difficulty finding parking.

## Second: Manshia (an important additional area near Downtown)

### Overview
Manshia is close to Downtown (a few minutes from Nabi Daniel), and is
Alexandria's main hub for wholesale and value-oriented retail.

### Suitable Businesses
- **Wholesale clothing/export-surplus retail** -- Manshia is historically
  known as a major hub for ready-made garments and export-surplus goods at
  wholesale-friendly prices, well suited to a clothing business targeting
  value-conscious or bulk-buying customers.
- **Electrical appliances, electronics, and phones** -- a well-known cluster
  of electronics and electrical-supply shops, creating a cluster effect that
  benefits any new phone or electrical appliance shop.

### Advantages
- Rents relatively lower than upscale Downtown.
- Strong cluster effect for the same business type (price comparison draws
  more customers).

### Challenges
- Value/wholesale character, not suited to a premium or boutique
  positioning.

## Third: Smouha

### Overview
Smouha is one of the upscale areas that combines residential and commercial
services, known for its residents' higher purchasing power. It's one of
Alexandria's largest and most commercially active districts, centered around
**Green Plaza Mall** and the area around Victor Emmanuel Square, Fawzy Moaz
Street, and the 14th of May corridor.

### Suitable Businesses
Cafés, restaurants, pharmacies, cosmetics, electrical appliances, clothing
stores.

### Advantages
- Middle-to-high income residents.
- Organized streets.
- Good safety level.

### Challenges
- High rents.
- Strong competition.

---

## Fourth: Sidi Gaber

### Overview
One of the most active areas, known for its proximity to transit stations
(Sidi Gaber train station -- a major transportation hub in the city),
universities, and hospitals, creating steady daily traffic from commuters
and office workers.

### Suitable Businesses
Cafés, restaurants, pharmacies, bakeries, phone shops, supermarkets.

### Advantages
- Continuous traffic throughout the day.
- Great diversity of customers.

### Challenges
- Congestion.
- Difficulty finding parking.

---

## Fifth: Miami

### Overview
One of the important residential and commercial areas in eastern Alexandria,
serving a large number of residents. Its most important streets include:

- **Khaled Ibn Al-Waleed Street** -- specifically known for beachwear and
  beach-related goods at good prices, popular with summer visitors, and also
  home to complementary fast-food, grocery, and café shops.
- **Gamal Abdel Nasser Street (Miami/Mandara area)** -- specifically known
  for mobile phone retail, a strong comparison-shopping cluster.

### Suitable Businesses
Supermarkets, pharmacies, bakeries, clothing stores, phone shops, herbal/
spice shops, produce shops, beachwear/beach goods (Khaled Ibn Al-Waleed
Street specifically).

### Advantages
- High population density.
- Continuous demand for daily-needs services.

### Challenges
- Moderate to high competition.

## Sixth: Asafra

### Overview
One of the largest residential areas in Alexandria, known for a large
resident population.

### Suitable Businesses
Supermarkets, pharmacies, bakeries, clothing stores, household goods shops,
produce shops.

### Advantages
- High population density.
- Lower rents than upscale areas.

### Challenges
- Purchasing power varies between streets.

---

## Seventh: San Stefano

### Overview
One of the upscale areas that includes commercial centers and high-income
residents, centered around **San Stefano Grand Plaza**, which includes
international fashion brands, upscale F&B, a marina, and a hotel.

### Suitable Businesses
Clothing, cosmetics, cafés, restaurants, gifts, electrical appliances.

### Advantages
- High purchasing power.
- Customers seeking quality.

### Challenges
- Very high rents.

---

## Eighth: Roushdy

### Overview
A vibrant district in the heart of the "Sharq" (East) area, one of the
city's most important commercial districts overall, home to a dense
concentration of shops, malls, and bank branches, and close to Pharos and
Alexandria universities.

### Suitable Businesses
Cafés, restaurants, clothing, phone shops, bookstores.

### Advantages
- Proximity to universities.
- Young population density.

---

## Ninth: Gleem

### Overview
A very upscale area, especially during the summer season, drawing summer
visitors and tourists thanks to its proximity to the corniche, which has
attracted growing investment in cafés and restaurants.

### Suitable Businesses
Cafés, restaurants, clothing, cosmetics.

### Advantages
- Upscale area.
- Proximity to the corniche.

---

## Tenth: Mandara

### Overview
Part of the same commercial zone as Miami, extended by Gamal Abdel Nasser
Street known for phone shops.

### Suitable Businesses
Supermarkets, pharmacies, bakeries, produce shops, clothing stores.

### Advantages
- High population density.
- Continuous commercial activity.

---

## Eleventh: Bahari

### Overview
A tourist area with a heritage character, high visitor numbers especially
during seasons.

### Suitable Businesses
Restaurants, cafés, gifts, heritage products, seafood shops.

### Advantages
- Tourist area.
- High seasonal demand.

---

## Twelfth: Moharam Bek

### Suitable Businesses
Workshops, car spare parts, electrical appliances, supermarkets,
pharmacies.

### Advantages
- Diverse commercial activity.
- Easy access.

---

## Thirteenth: El Hadra

### Suitable Businesses
Carpentry workshops, craft workshops, spare parts, warehouses, household
goods.

### Advantages
- Suited to industrial and craft businesses.

---

## Fourteenth: Borg El Arab

### Suitable Businesses
Workshops, factories, warehouses, building materials, logistics services.

### Advantages
- Industrial area.
- Large spaces.
- Suited to production-oriented businesses.

---

## Fifteenth: Souq El-Sa'a (an additional area for home textiles)

### Overview
A specialized market known for home textiles, curtains, and bridal
trousseau shopping.

### Suitable Businesses
Tailor/sewing shops (especially those offering home-textile services),
home-textile and curtain shops.

### Advantages
- Customers are already in a "shopping mode" for home goods and furnishings,
  benefiting any complementary business.

## How Does the AI Choose the Right Area?

The system relies on several factors provided by the user, such as:
- Type of business.
- Available budget.
- Target demographic.
- Required size.
- Does the business depend on foot traffic or vehicle traffic?
- Does it target area residents or visitors?

It then compares these requirements with the characteristics of different
areas (including income level and the specialized streets within each
area), and suggests more than one suitable area with reasons for the
recommendation -- citing a specific street name if mentioned in this guide
(such as Fouad Street or Khaled Ibn Al-Waleed Street), rather than settling
for just the general area name.

## Conclusion

There's no single area that's best for all businesses -- choosing a
location depends on the nature of the business, the budget, the target
customers, the level of competition, and the income level in the area. It's
always recommended to study more than one area and compare their advantages
before making a rental decision.

## Disclaimer and Maintenance Note

This guide is built on general knowledge and research from publicly
available sources about Alexandria's commercial areas, not a formal field
market survey. Prices and the "hot" streets for each business type change
over time. The product team should review and refresh this guide
periodically, ideally with input from local real-estate brokers or active
landlords in each area, before treating it as a definitive reference for any
rental decision.
